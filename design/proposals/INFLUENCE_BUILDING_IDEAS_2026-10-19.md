# Influence-building ideas, pinned for later (2026-10-19)

Status: **ideas only, nothing built.** Cameron asked to pin these and revisit them after Patron Bills step 3 (the runtime level) is done and merged. Both are ways to build influence on one bill during its level, on top of the standing and meta gates that unlock the vote swing today.

## 1. A member of parliament visits, then you battle them

| Part | Idea |
|---|---|
| Shape | A special stage sequence: another MP visits to discuss the bill, then you fight them one on one |
| Part A (visitor-like) | A short series of questions, like an Office Hours visit. The answers raise or lower the **starting support** of the battle that follows |
| Part B (battle) | An ordinary one-on-one battle against that MP |
| Prize | Winning the member over is a fixed **+1 influence** to that member's **party**, **for this bill's vote only** |
| First targets | Five Point Independents and Yezo Heritage Party |

Questions to settle later: whether +1 means one more swing seat or one more gate point; how the stage is slotted into a level template (a new template token); whether a lost battle costs anything; how many such visits a bill can have; which MPs are eligible (the party's own MPs from the Opponents tab).

## 2. A lobbyist offers a trade: drop a component

| Part | Idea |
|---|---|
| Shape | A special stage where a lobbyist visits with a proposal to **drop one of the bill's components** (the player chooses which) |
| In exchange | One or more of: **help work the bill** (more influence on this bill); **sentiment** with the lobbyist's affiliated organization(s); **favor** with one or more political parties; an **office donation** (Funds and XP) |
| How much | Depends on how the player negotiates: a **negotiation leverage indicator** (a visible meter the answers move) decides how many of the rewards are granted and how big |

Questions to settle later: what builds leverage (rhetorical style choices, standing with the lobbyist's organization, the number of parties already leaning your way); whether dropping a component changes the bill's value tags, positions and law effects at once (it would, so the vote preview must be recomputed); which components may be dropped (never the last one; the patron's core one costs standing with the patron); how this interacts with Amend and with Option A's already-enacted swaps.

Both would be new template tokens in Bill Level Templates and new stage families, so they need the step 3 runtime level first.
