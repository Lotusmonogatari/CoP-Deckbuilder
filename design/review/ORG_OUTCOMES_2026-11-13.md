## Reading the tables

- **One organization stands out: Taxpayers' Alliance.** In event choices it takes 14 negative outcomes against 6 positive (70% negative, against 41% overall, z about +2.6), loses 15 standing points for every 6 it gains, and in **10 of the 16 events it appears in it only ever loses**. It is also the most frequently appearing organization, so it is both the most over-represented and the one most often on the losing side.
- **No other organization is flagged in events.** Chamber of Commerce, Financial Industry Association and Innovation Society also lean negative (56% to 67%), but each has only 3 to 9 outcomes, too few to call a pattern.
- **Net standing across all events:** the biggest net losers are Taxpayers' Alliance (-9), Our Traditional Society (-8), Small Business Owners' Association (-6) and Ainu Community Council (-6). The biggest net winners are Neighborhood Associations (+6), Family Network (+3) and Local Kōenkai (+3).
- **Visitors:** only Neighborhood Associations differ, and in the good direction (23% negative against 43% overall, +12 net).
- **Visitor questions are not a fair measure.** Each question has one right answer and three wrong ones, so 59% of all outcomes are negative by construction. The four flagged organizations there (National Media, Financial Industry Association, Members of Parliament, Constituency Voters) are *less* negative than the rest, which mostly reflects what the questions ask about, not bias.
- **Event choices are trade-offs.** A choice that pleases one organization usually displeases another, so some negative share is by design (41% overall). The question is whether the same organizations always pay, which is what Taxpayers' Alliance shows.

## Limits

- Counts are signed standing changes on organizations only; meta rewards (Funds, Reputation) and awareness are not included.
- Promise-broken penalties are conditional (they only happen if the player breaks a promise) but are counted as outcomes, so the "immediate rewards only" table is the stricter one. It gives the same finding.
- Event choices are not weighted by how often a player will see them.
