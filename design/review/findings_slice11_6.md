# Findings: slice11_6 (Room Questions S1668-S2076, 409 rows)

The slice is 51 themes x 8 templated questions. Each template carries an identical grade vector in every theme, and every row has `asked by None` and no stage list. Template flaws are therefore reported once, with the IDs they hit.

Template key (offset within each theme block of 8):
- T1 "Who pays if we {x}?" (Ear S, Emo M, App M, Dat S, Div W, Dup M)
- T2 "Why should Yezo {x}?"
- T3 "Critics say we should not {x}. What do you tell them?"
- T4 "{Org} wants us to {x}. Why should everyone else care?"
- T5 "What would it mean for my town if we {x}?"
- T6 "How would you make sure we {x} fairly and on schedule?"
- T7 "What goes wrong if we {x} badly?"
- T8 "Is there a smaller way than to {x}?"

ROWS READ: 409 of 409

Summary of the biggest patterns:
1. All 51 themes share eight templates with identical grade vectors. The T8 "Is there a smaller way than to ..." question is ungrammatical, the T6 fairness question gives Duplicitous a strong grade, and T5 "my town" and T1 "Who pays" fail on national or non-fiscal themes. Every row has no asker.
2. Pleases/displeases pairs are often unjustified. Four are contradictory (S1729-S1736, S1833-S1848, S1937-S1944), and the Chamber, Labor, Innovation, Students United and Rural Revitalization pairings are repeated across themes without a reason.
3. Plural organization names take a singular verb and repeat the theme (S1788, S1796, S1812; Agricultural Cooperatives). "Hokkaido ports" (S1745-S1752) introduces a real-world place name into a fictional nation.
