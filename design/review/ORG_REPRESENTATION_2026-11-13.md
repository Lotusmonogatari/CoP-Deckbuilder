## Reading the table

- **Party organizations** (Party Headquarters, Party Faction, Party Activists, Candidate Selection Committee) have almost no events: they are moved by dues, party standing and the Steering Committee, not by Office events. Whether they should have events is a design call.
- **Members of Parliament** and **Constituency Voters** are audience groups reached mostly through visits and questions, so a low event count is expected.
- **Visitors** are far more even than events: only two organizations have none, and nine have 20 or more. **Events** are the lopsided part: Neighborhood Associations (15) and Taxpayers' Alliance (16) hold about a quarter of all event touches.
- **Taxpayers' Alliance** is over-represented mainly through visitor questions (183 of 1,504 touches, the most of any organization), while it has only 8 visitors of its own.
- **"Named in text"** is low for every organization (0 to 2 events), so the structured targets are the real signal; the authors rarely name an organization in an event's own words.

## Limits

- Touches are structured effect targets, so an event that is *about* an organization but rewards others is not counted, and an event touching three organizations counts for all three. A heavier event (a larger delta) counts the same as a light one.
- Random-event exposure also depends on weight and on awareness and season rules, which are not weighted here beyond the "Random weight" column.
- Followup and Buyable events count the same as Random ones.
